# Set operations on real read sets — results

Protocol, data and machine: [`EXPERIMENT.md`](EXPERIMENT.md). The bucket-count follow-up and the
construction bug it exposed (fixed in `2167acb`): [`BUCKET_SWEEP.md`](BUCKET_SWEEP.md). Tables
regenerated by `python3 drivers/summarize.py` from `data/*.csv`. All 288 set-op rows are
cardinality-checked against KMC (`verified = ok`).

## Summary

- **Volume.** Each side holds 0.5–4.0 G distinct k-mers, and |A∪B| reaches 6.6 G (ocean k=31).
  That is 3 to 24× the chr1 point of the deck. The pairs span J = 0.15–0.62.
- **t=8 (sklib default `--buckets 4096`).**
  - ∩, A∖B and B∖A: sklib is 2.5–7.9× faster than KMC. KMC pays a flat price per pair, while
    sklib pays for the result.
  - Union: sklib is 1.1–2.4× faster.
  - Joint (four outputs): from 0.81× (ocean k=31, the only loss) to 1.76×.
  - The lead grows at k=63, where KMC databases are 16 B/k-mer.
- **t=1.** KMC is 1.7–4.5× faster on the union and the joint. `kmc_tools -t1` keeps ≈ 2.3–3.9
  cores busy (%CPU column), while sklib uses one.
- **Scaling.** At t=8, sklib's union throughput falls with size: from 96 Mk-mer/s on chr1 to 53 on
  ocean k=31, while KMC stays around 40–50. The bucket sweep traces most of this to the default
  4096 buckets. 65536 buckets give −23 to −31 % on the ocean unions and joints (`BUCKET_SWEEP.md`).
- **Memory.**
  - sklib's peak RSS for set ops is 20 MB–2.3 GB (t=8) against KMC's 1.8–3.2 GB.
  - The "20–100× less memory" of chr1 shrinks at this scale, because sklib's per-worker buffers
    grow with the bucket size.
  - In construction: sklib needs 0.6–2.0 GB, KMC 11.2 GB.
- **Index size.** sklib uses 19–23 bits/k-mer, KMC 56 (k=31) and 120 (k=63): 2.4–6.4× smaller.
- **Construction (t=8).** KMC is 2.8–4.5× faster, while chr1 had only 1.35×. On reads, every
  occurrence of a k-mer, duplicates included, goes through sklib's phase 1.
- **Drift.** The chr1 anchor ran 5–7 % faster at the end than at the start, for both tools alike,
  so the comparison within the campaign is unbiased.
